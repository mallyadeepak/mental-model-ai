About Worry Compass

Worry Compass is a small, private tool for practicing one skill: telling the difference between a worry and a problem, then handling each one appropriately. Anxious thought tends to blur that distinction — everything feels urgent and unsolvable at once. Separating the two is a large part of what makes worry manageable.

The method

  • Name the thought. Writing a worry down, specifically, already reduces its grip — vague dread is harder to sit with than a concrete sentence.
  • Check for thinking patterns. Ten common cognitive distortions (Aaron Beck, David Burns) — catastrophizing, fortune-telling, mind reading, and others — quietly inflate how bad or likely something seems. Naming the pattern makes it easier to question.
  • Ask if it's actionable. This is the core move from CBT-based worry management (Borkovec, Leahy): is this a present, near-term problem you can influence, or a hypothetical / uncontrollable "what if"? The two need completely different responses.
  • If actionable — solve it. A short structured version of Problem-Solving Therapy (D'Zurilla & Nezu): define the problem precisely, generate options without filtering, choose one, and commit to a specific next step with a time and place attached (an "implementation intention," shown to meaningfully increase follow-through).
  • If not actionable — set it down. Distinguishing what's in your control from what isn't, and deliberately postponing or releasing the rest, is a practiced skill (related to worry-postponement techniques and the classic "dichotomy of control"), not the same thing as suppression or avoidance.
  • Bank the evidence. Self-confidence isn't built by reassurance — it's built from mastery experiences: specific memories of "I said I'd do something hard, and I did it" (Albert Bandura's self-efficacy theory). The evidence bank exists to make those memories easy to find again.

Your data

Everything you write is stored only in this browser's local storage, on this device. There is no account, no server, and no analytics — nothing you write is ever sent anywhere. Clearing your browser data will erase it, so consider that if you want to keep a long history.

A note on scope

This is a self-reflection and practice tool, not therapy, diagnosis, or medical advice, and it isn't a substitute for working with a qualified therapist or psychiatrist — especially if worry is severe, persistent, or affecting daily functioning. If you're in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please contact a mental health professional or a crisis line right away — in the US, you can call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline).
